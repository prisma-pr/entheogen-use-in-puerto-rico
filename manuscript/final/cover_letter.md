We submit our manuscript, "Ceremonial Entheogen Use in Puerto Rico: A Descriptive Survey of Participants
and a Small Facilitator Subsample," for consideration as an Original Research article in the *Journal of
Psychoactive Drugs*.

**What the study is.** We conducted an anonymous, bilingual, cross-sectional online survey of adults aged
21 and over who reported prior involvement in entheogen-assisted ceremonies in Puerto Rico, fielded 24
February–9 June 2026 by convenience and chain-referral sampling (N = 73 analytic sample, of 100
submissions). The survey describes who participates, how ceremonies are structured, what preparation and
screening practices participants and facilitators report, satisfaction with the most recent ceremony, and
a safety domain covering non-consensual physical contact and facilitator-witnessed crisis signs. A small
facilitator subsample (n = 8, 7 of whom are also participants) is described separately and cannot be
statistically linked to the participants they served, since the instrument does not connect a participant
to the specific ceremony or facilitator involved.

**Why it fits the journal's scope.** *Journal of Psychoactive Drugs* regularly publishes naturalistic,
survey-based descriptions of ceremonial and community psychedelic/entheogen use outside clinical-trial
settings — including closely comparable recent work such as the ayahuasca-ceremony-attender survey from
Portugal published in this journal. Our manuscript extends that line of survey-based, non-clinical
description to a Spanish-speaking Caribbean jurisdiction that has not previously been characterized this
way.

**The novelty claim.** To the authors' knowledge, this is the first descriptive characterization of
ceremonial (as opposed to solitary or clinical) entheogen use practices in Puerto Rico. One prior
Puerto Rico-specific survey exists (Vélez Rodríguez et al., cited in the manuscript) but characterizes
psilocybin use patterns and personality correlates without describing how ceremonies are conducted, and
an unpublished thesis examined student perceptions of psilocybin mushrooms without collecting ceremonial
practice or safety data. No prior study describes ceremony structure, preparation, screening, or safety
outcomes for this population.

**An honest statement of the study's limits.** This is a descriptive, non-probability, unweighted survey
of a self-selected sample. It supports no claim about the prevalence of ceremonial entheogen use in
Puerto Rico and no causal inference about what any practice produces; its principal contribution is
descriptive and instrument-level, identifying what a properly powered successor study would need to
measure, sample, and correct. The manuscript states this explicitly in the abstract, Discussion, and
Conclusion, and we ask that reviewers evaluate it on those terms.

**Originality and concurrent submission.** This manuscript is original, has not been published previously
in whole or in part, and is not under consideration by any other journal. All authors have reviewed and
approved the submitted version and agree to its submission to *Journal of Psychoactive Drugs*.

in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors. Several authors are affiliated with organizations

and title page.
