## Two PR-specific prior items found

### 1. Vélez Rodríguez, Duquesne-Maldonado, Hernández Torres, Da'Luz Sant'Ana & Nazario (2026)
"Psilocybin use in Puerto Rico: Patterns, motivations, and personality correlates from an online survey."
*Journal of Psychedelic Studies*, Online First, published 2026-04-15.
DOI: [10.1556/2054.2026.00519](https://doi.org/10.1556/2054.2026.00519) (verified via CrossRef + Semantic
Scholar).

- IRB approval: University of Puerto Rico Medical Sciences Campus (RCM) — same institution collaborating on
  this project.
- Design: cross-sectional online survey, n=343, convenience/internet-advertisement recruitment.
- Scope: **single substance (psilocybin only)**. Lifetime use 52.6%; motivations (curiosity 43%, spiritual
  expansion 69%); TIPI-SPA personality correlates (openness aOR=2.09, agreeableness aOR=1.75); sex/gender and
  sexual-orientation differences (male aOR=6.2, bisexual aOR=3.14); safety/addiction perceptions (57% believed
  safe, 61% believed non-addictive); no ceremonial/facilitation, preparation, screening, or non-consensual-
  contact content — that domain is entirely outside this paper's scope.
- **Author-confirmed 2026-08-13: the corresponding/first author is the same research team as this project**
  (self-identified via AskUserQuestion during this session). This is a companion output, not an independent
  competing prior-work finding — treat as a self-citation to be coordinated explicitly, not a threat to the
  novelty claim in the usual sense.

### 2. Doelter Morales & Martínez Miranda (2020)
"Hongos mágicos...más allá de las alucinaciones: Análisis político-social y estudio exploratorio del
conocimiento y las percepciones que tiene la comunidad de la Universidad de Puerto Rico, Recinto de Río
Piedras sobre el uso de los hongos psicodélicos a diciembre de 2020." M.A. thesis, University of Puerto Rico,
Río Piedras. Repository: https://repositorio.upr.edu/handle/11721/2540

- Mixed-methods: Google Forms survey + qualitative phase, recruited via campus flyers/social media.
- Scope: **knowledge and perceptions** of psilocybin mushrooms among the UPR Río Piedras university community
  (not a PR-wide adult sample), covering therapeutic/recreational/cultural/spiritual/religious discourse
  frames. Not a use-pattern or ceremonial-practice survey; unpublished thesis, not peer-reviewed.
- Distinct enough in population (campus community vs. PR-wide adults 21+), design (perceptions vs. use
  practice), and substance scope (psilocybin only vs. multi-substance) that it does not by itself block a
  ceremonial-use novelty claim, but it should be cited as evidence of prior PR-specific interest in this space.

## Conclusion — novelty framing decision (resolved with author 2026-08-13)

**No prior published survey of ceremonial/facilitated, multi-substance entheogen use practices in Puerto
Rico exists.** Both PR-specific items found are narrower in scope (single-substance psilocybin; general
use-pattern or perceptions focus, not ceremony structure, facilitation, preparation/screening, or safety).

**Decision:** narrow the manuscript's novelty claim from an unqualified "first survey of entheogen use in
Puerto Rico" to **"first survey specifically characterizing ceremonial/facilitated entheogen use practices
(preparation, screening, facilitator role, safety) in Puerto Rico."** Cite Vélez Rodríguez et al. (2026)
explicitly in the Introduction as a companion/concurrent PR-specific psychedelic-survey output from an
overlapping research team, with an explicit sentence distinguishing scope (single-substance use-pattern and
personality correlates vs. this study's multi-substance ceremonial-practice and safety focus) rather than
letting the overlap surface only in the Discussion or literature-search appendix. Cite Doelter Morales &
Martínez Miranda (2020) as supporting evidence of an existing but distinct PR research interest in this
space (perceptions, not ceremonial practice).

No Caribbean-region ceremonial-entheogen survey of any design was found; the two Jamaica items are clinical
psilocybin-assisted-therapy case studies (comparator literature, §2c), not a design precedent for this
population/method.
