Taylor & Francis requires authors who use generative AI tools during research or manuscript preparation to disclose this transparently.

### Required Wording and Declaration Details
The declaration statement must explicitly include:
1. The **full name of the tool** used (including the **version number**).
2. **How** the tool was used.
3. The **reason** for using the tool.

If generative AI was **not** used, Taylor & Francis suggests the following wording: 
*"The authors report generative AI was not used in their research or preparation of this manuscript."*

### Required Manuscript Placement
- **Journal Article Submissions:** A specific AI usage disclosure statement must be included within the article, typically in the **Methods** or **Acknowledgments** section (or as a specific AI usage disclosure statement section as specified by the journal).
- **Book Manuscripts:** Authors must disclose their intent to use generative AI tools to their editorial contact at the earliest possible stage (at proposal or manuscript writing phase) for approval. If approved, the declaration statement must be placed in the **Preface** or **Introduction** of the book.

### Key Policies & Rules
- **No AI Authorship:** Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) **must not be listed as an author** because they cannot take legal accountability or manage copyright/licensing agreements.
- **Author Accountability:** Human authors remain fully responsible for the originality, validity, and integrity of the content, including verifying the accuracy of all text, references, and citations generated.
